History

Lebanon has a rich history spanning thousands of years as a crossroads of Mediterranean and Middle Eastern civilizations

Phoenicians:

From 3200 to 539 BC, coastal cities like Byblos, Sidon, and Tyre formed Phoenicia, a famous maritime trading culture that spread across the Mediterranean.

Foreign Rule:

The region fell under Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Greek, Roman, and Byzantine control.

French Mandate:

Following the collapse of the Ottoman Empire, the League of Nations placed the region under French control in 1920, creating the State of Greater Lebanon.

Independence:

Lebanon declared independence in 1943, establishing a unique power-sharing government divided among its various religious communities.

Civil War

A devastating civil war lasted from 1975 to 1990, involving various domestic factions and regional powers. It ended with the Taif Agreement.
